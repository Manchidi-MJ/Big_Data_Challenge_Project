## Slide 1 — Title & team
**Content:** Project title; team members and roles; module; date.
**Notes (all):** Introduce the project: understanding and predicting e-commerce
customer churn using big-data tooling, RFM segmentation and ML, on a 10k-customer
Kaggle dataset.

## Slide 2 — Business context & problem statement
**Content:** Why churn matters; retailers struggle to spot at-risk customers
before they leave; retention is an important (but context-dependent) lever.
**Notes (MA):** Set up the business motivation and state the problem: behavioural
indicators exist but their relationship to churn isn't obvious without structured
analysis.

## Slide 3 — Research question & objectives
**Content:** Main question (behaviours most associated with churn; how accurately
ML can predict it) + 3 supporting questions + 7 objectives (condensed).
**Notes (JM):** Frame the questions we'll answer and preview that the ML answer
is nuanced because of a data issue we discovered.

## Slide 4 — Dataset, provenance & limitations
**Content:** Kaggle *E-Commerce Customer Churn 2025 – 10K*; MIT licence;
10,000 × 15 verified; one row per customer; no missing/duplicates; churn 37.7%;
provenance (real vs synthetic) unverified.
**Notes (TH):** Stress we verified the real schema (it differed from the brief's
provisional list) and that we're honest about not confirming provenance.

## Slide 5 — Data engineering & PySpark pipeline
**Content:** Reproducible pipeline (read → dedupe → impute → normalise → derive
tenure → persist); pandas + PySpark parity; honest note that 10k rows aren't
"big" — PySpark demonstrates a scalable design.
**Notes (TH):** Explain the learn-once-run-anywhere design and the Windows
Parquet/winutils note; nothing was dropped (10k → 10k).

## Slide 6 — EDA & churn distribution
**Figure:** `01_churn_distribution.png` (+ mention `05_correlation_heatmap.png`).
**Content:** 37.7% churn; churned vs retained differ almost only in recency
(≈ 9 vs ≈ 301 days); other features nearly identical.
**Notes (MA):** Walk through the headline EDA pattern: recency dominates;
demographics/spend don't separate churners.

## Slide 7 — RFM segmentation
**Figure:** RFM segment table / bar of churn-by-segment.
**Content:** True RFM from recency/orders/spend; segments; "Needs Attention"
(31.8%) and "At Risk" (8.3%) ≈ 94% churn; recent segments ≈ 0%.
**Notes (MA):** Explain RFM scoring (5 bins, recency reversed) and that segments
split cleanly by recency.

## Slide 8 — Machine-learning approach
**Content:** Binary classification; leakage-aware pipelines; Dummy / LR / RF;
stratified split, seed 42, CV for selection; two experiments (A = all, B =
leakage-resistant).
**Notes (JM):** Emphasise preprocessing inside the pipeline (no leakage from
scaling/encoding) and why we designed two experiments.

## Slide 9 — Leakage finding (the key result)
**Figure:** `05_correlation_heatmap.png` (churn↔days_since = 0.84).
**Content:** `churned == (days_since_last_purchase > 90)` at 100% accuracy →
target-defining leakage.
**Notes (JM):** This is the project's most important result and the reason the
"best" naive model is not real prediction.

## Slide 10 — Model evaluation & comparison
**Figure:** `roc_experiment_B.png` + comparison table.
**Content:** Exp A ~100% (leakage); Exp B RF ROC-AUC 0.54, F1 0.37; LR F1 0.46;
barely above Dummy (acc 0.623).
**Notes (JM):** Contrast A vs B; discuss precision/recall cost for retention;
conclude no honest predictive power from non-recency behaviour.

## Slide 11 — Feature interpretation & retention recommendations
**Figure:** `importance_rf_expb.png`.
**Content:** Exp B importances all tiny; recommendations R1–R4 (re-engage before
day 90; protect Champions; prioritise At Risk; don't deploy a predictive model
yet). Each tied to a result; associations not causation.
**Notes (MA + JM):** Keep findings separate from proposed actions; note we can't
claim causation.

## Slide 12 — Conclusion, limitations & future work
**Content:** Deliverables; leakage is the headline; dataset describes the base
but can't predict future churn; future: event-level forward-labelled data, A/B
test, scale-test Spark. Thank you / questions.
**Notes (all):** Summarise the honest story and invite questions.
